## 1. Global CEFR A1 Descriptors (Can-Do Statements)
A successful A1 learner can:
1. **Hören (Listening):** Understand familiar everyday words and very basic phrases concerning themselves, their family, and immediate concrete surroundings when people speak slowly and clearly.
2. **Lesen (Reading):** Understand familiar names, words, and very simple sentences (e.g., on notices, posters, short emails, menus, and timetables).
3. **Sprechen (Spoken Interaction & Production):**
   - *Interaction:* Interact in a simple way provided the other person is prepared to repeat or rephrase things at a slower rate of speech. Ask and answer simple questions in areas of immediate need or on very familiar topics.
   - *Production:* Use simple phrases and sentences to describe where they live, people they know, and everyday activities.
4. **Schreiben (Writing):** Write a short, simple postcard or message (e.g., sending holiday greetings) and fill in forms with personal details (e.g., name, nationality, address on a hotel registration form).

## 2. Comprehensive 10-Module Curriculum Matrix

| Module | Title (DE / KA) | Everyday Domain | CEFR Can-Do Outcomes | Core Grammar Focus | Contrastive Georgian Nuances |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lektion 1** | *Erste Kontakte & Begrüßung* / პირველი კონტაქტი, გაცნობა და ანბანი | Introductions, greetings, spelling names, nationality | Can introduce self, ask names, spell names, ask origins, use polite address (*Sie*) vs casual (*du*). | Verb conjugation in Präsens (*heißen, kommen, sein, sprechen*); W-Fragen & Ja/Nein-Fragen; V2 word order rule. | Copula omission: Georgian drops "არის" casually, but German *always* requires `sein` (*Ich bin Luka*); inverted word order (*Aus Georgien komme ich*). |
| **Lektion 2** | *Familie, Freunde & Zahlen* / ოჯახი, მეგობრები და რიცხვები | Family tree, relationships, age, phone numbers, marital status | Can name family members, talk about age and relations, exchange contact info, describe marital status (*ledig, verheiratet*). | Grammatical gender: *der, die, das*; Possessive pronouns: *mein/meine, dein/deine, sein, ihr*; Numbers 0–100. | Georgian lacks grammatical gender; German masculine/feminine/neuter articles must be memorized with nouns; German numbers say units before tens (*fünfundzwanzig*). |
| **Lektion 3** | *Essen, Trinken & Einkaufen* / საჭმელი, სასმელი და საყიდლები | Groceries, food, supermarket, prices, restaurant orders | Can order food and drinks, ask prices (*Was kostet...?*), request quantities (*ein Kilo, eine Flasche*), express preferences (*gern / nicht gern*). | Indefinite article: *ein, eine, ein*; Negation: *kein* (for nouns) vs *nicht* (for verbs/adjectives); Plural forms (*-e, -er, -en, -s*, Umlaut). | Georgian uses single negation "არ/არა", German strictly distinguishes noun negation (*Ich habe keinen Apfel*) from verb/adjective negation (*Das ist nicht billig*). |
| **Lektion 4** | *Meine Wohnung & Der Akkusativ* / ჩემი ბინა და ბრალდებითი ბრუნვა | Housing, rooms, furniture, rent, descriptions | Can describe an apartment, name rooms and furniture, evaluate size and light (*hell, dunkel, groß*), understand rental terms (*Warmmiete*). | Accusative case (*Der Akkusativ*): *den / einen / keinen* for masculine nouns; Adjective predicative use; spatial vocabulary. | Georgian direct object (სახელობითი/მოთხრობითი) vs German Akkusativ where only masculine changes (*der -> den*), feminine and neuter remain unchanged. |
| **Lektion 5** | *Tagesablauf & Trennbare Verben* / დღის რეჟიმი, საათი და განცალკევებადი ზმნები | Daily routines, schedule, telling time, appointments | Can tell time (official 24h & colloquial 12h), schedule appointments (*einen Termin vereinbaren*), describe daily routine (*aufstehen, frühstücken*). | Separable verbs (*trennbare Verben*); Prepositions of time (*um, am, im*); Verb bracket in main clause (*Satzklammer*). | Separable prefix moves strictly to the end of the clause (*Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf*); "halb zehn" means 09:30 (halfway to 10), contrasting with Georgian "ათის ნახევარი". |
| **Lektion 6** | *Freizeit, Hobbys & Modalverben* / თავისუფალი დრო და მოდალური ზმნები | Hobbies, sports, leisure, weekend plans, invitations | Can talk about hobbies, accept/decline invitations, express abilities (*können*) and wishes (*wollen, möchten*), state frequency (*oft, manchmal*). | Modal verbs (*können, wollen, möchten*); Modal verb syntax (conjugated modal at pos. 2, infinitive at sentence end); Days of the week. | Modal verb frame: infinitive is strictly sentence-final, unlike Georgian where verbs can sit adjacent; vowel gradation in modal singular (*ich kann, du kannst*). |
| **Lektion 7** | *Unterwegs in der Stadt & Der Dativ* / ქალაქში ორიენტაცია და მიცემითი ბრუნვა | City navigation, transportation, asking directions, public transport | Can ask and give directions (*geradeaus, nach links/rechts*), ask about public transit (*Bus, U-Bahn, Zug*), buy tickets. | Dative case (*Der Dativ*): *dem, der, dem, den (+n)*; Dative prepositions (*mit, nach, zu, bei, aus, von, seit*); Question word *Wo?*. | Feminine article *die* becomes *der* in Dative (*mit der U-Bahn*), often confusing Georgians who associate *der* solely with masculine Nominativ. |
| **Lektion 8** | *Beruf, Arbeit & Alltag* / პროფესია, სამუშაო და მოდალური ზმნები | Occupations, workplace, working hours, rules & obligations | Can name professions, describe job duties, state workplace (*Ich arbeite bei... als...*), understand workplace rules (*müssen, dürfen*). | Modal verbs of obligation & permission (*müssen, dürfen - nicht dürfen*); Feminine profession suffix *-in*; Prepositions *als* and *bei*. | Distinction between *müssen* (must) and *nicht dürfen* (strictly prohibited), contrasting with Georgian ambiguous "არ უნდა" (doesn't have to vs must not). |
| **Lektion 9** | *Körper, Gesundheit & Imperativ* / სხეული, ჯანმრთელობა და ბრძანებითი კილო | Body parts, illness, doctor's visit, pharmacy, giving advice | Can describe physical symptoms and pain (*Der Kopf tut weh*), understand doctor's instructions, give simple advice (*sollen*), give polite commands. | Imperative mood (*Der Imperativ: du, ihr, Sie*); Modal verb *sollen* for medical advice; Reflexive/body expressions (*wehtun, Schmerzen haben*). | German polite imperative retains the pronoun (*Trinken Sie viel Wasser!*), whereas informal *du* drops both pronoun and ending (*Trink!*). |
| **Lektion 10** | *Urlaub & Das Perfekt* / შვებულება და წარსული დრო | Vacations, travel, past experiences, storytelling | Can talk about past events, weekend trips, vacations, describe travel activities, write a simple holiday email. | Past conversational tense (*Das Perfekt*): *haben* vs *sein* auxiliary verbs; Partizip II forms (regular *ge-...-t*, irregular *ge-...-en*, separable *auf-ge-standen*). | Past tense construction: Georgian uses synthetic aorist/imperfect, German conversational past uses compound auxiliary + past participle at the end (*Ich habe ein Buch gekauft* / *Ich bin geflogen*). |

## 3. Four Core Skills Implementation Plan (Stages 2–5)
- **Hören Lab:** Authentic audio recordings with speech rate modulation (1.0x / 0.7x), sequential line playback, transcription hidden until submission.
- **Lesen Lab:** Realia texts (emails, announcements, schedules, receipts) with factual comprehension and vocabulary callouts.
- **Schreiben Lab:** Real-world A1 tasks (form filling, meeting arrangement emails, complaint notes) with structured rubric guidance and self-check checklists.
- **Sprechen Lab:** Interactive prompts for guided oral introduction, situational dialogues, audio recording via browser MediaRecorder API, self-playback, and model comparison.
